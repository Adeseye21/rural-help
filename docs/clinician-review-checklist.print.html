Purpose: systematic sign-off of every health-guidance string the app shows to patients, before field testing or pilot. Review in order: Part A (emergencies) first, then the fever-with-body-aches pathway (B10).

Part B — Symptom pathways (non-emergency answers)

Every answer below ends with the disclaimer: "Rural Help suggestion: this is information, not a diagnosis. A healthcare professional should assess you to determine the cause."

B1. Fever

Fever is most often caused by an infection.

It can also be caused by other conditions, so a professional should decide the cause.

Questions: how high (Low / High / Very high / don't know); how long (under 1 day / 1–3 days / more than 3 days / on and off); stiff neck or rash (No / Stiff neck / Rash / Both).

Next step: "These symptoms need to be checked so the cause can be found. Consider contact with a healthcare professional or a visit to a facility."

Rest in a cool room, keep drinking fluids, and record your temperature if you can. Seek care if a fever lasts more than three days, if a baby under three months has a fever, or if you have a stiff neck or a rash that does not fade.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

B2. Cough

A cough is commonly caused by an infection or irritation in the airways.

Smoke and dust can also cause coughing.

Questions: duration (under 1 week / 1–3 weeks / more than 3 weeks); coughing anything up (Nothing / Clear mucus / Coloured mucus / Blood); fever as well (No / mild / high).

Warm drinks and staying away from smoke can help. Seek care if a cough lasts more than three weeks, brings up blood, or comes with breathlessness or chest pain.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

B3. Headache

Headaches have many possible causes, including stress, dehydration, and illness.

An unusual or very strong headache should be assessed by a professional.

Questions: sudden onset; worst headache ever; recent head injury.

Rest in a quiet dark room, drink water, and take any medicine you already use as advised. Seek care for a sudden severe headache, a headache with weakness or vision change, or one that keeps returning.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

B4. Stomach pain

Stomach pain can have several possible causes, including infection, food, and other conditions.

Severe or worsening pain needs professional assessment.

Questions: location (upper / lower / all over / right / left); strength (Mild / Moderate / Severe / Getting worse); vomiting (No / once or twice / many times).

Sips of clean water and rest can help while your stomach settles. Seek care for severe or one-sided pain, pain with a fever, blood in vomit or stool, or vomiting that will not stop.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

B5. Diarrhoea

Diarrhea is often caused by an infection or something you ate.

The main risk is dehydration, so drinking enough water matters.

Questions: duration; blood in stool; very thirsty or urinating less.

Keep drinking small sips of clean water or ORS to replace what you lose. Seek care for blood in stool, high fever, severe tummy pain, or if a child becomes weak or drowsy.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

B6. Injury (wound, cut, fall, burn, sprain, bruise, bleeding)

An injury may need examination to check for deeper damage.

Keeping the area clean and protected reduces the chance of infection.

Questions: how it happened (Fall / Cut or scrape / Burn / Hit / Other); numbness, weakness, or bone showing; tetanus vaccination up to date.

Clean a wound with clean water and cover it. Seek care if a bite or dirty wound is involved, if you cannot move or feel a limb, or if bleeding will not stop.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

B7. Rash

A rash can be caused by an allergy, an infection, or skin irritation.

A professional can examine a rash to identify the cause.

Questions: spreading quickly; fever as well.

Keep the skin cool and clean and do not scratch. Seek care if the rash does not fade when pressed, if you have a fever, if it spreads quickly, or if your face or throat swell.

B8. Tiredness

Tiredness has many causes, including illness, poor sleep, and stress.

Long-lasting or unexplained tiredness should be discussed with a professional.

Questions: duration (days / weeks / more than a month); sleeping and resting enough.

Sleep, eat regular meals, and drink enough water. Seek care if tiredness lasts more than two weeks, or comes with fever, weight loss, chest pain, or swelling in your legs.

B9. Body ache / joint pain on its own

Body aches and joint pains have many possible causes, including infection, inflammation, overwork, or a strain.

Pain in one joint that becomes hot, red, and swollen, or pain after an injury, needs professional assessment.

Questions: where (all over / one part / joints / muscles); how long (under 1 day / 1–3 days / more than 3 days / longer than 1 month); swollen, red, or hot (No / Yes / not sure); can move it normally (normally / a little / cannot).

Next step: routine check wording (no same-day demand for body ache alone).

Rest the sore part, keep moving gently so the joint does not stiffen, and drink plenty of water. Seek care if one joint becomes hot, red, or swollen, if you cannot move a limb, if a foot or leg feels cold, pale, or numb, or if the pain follows an injury.

Part D — First-aid guides (shown in emergencies)

Note above every guide: "Rural Help suggestion: this is basic first-aid information. It does not replace training or professional care. In an emergency, get professional help first."

D1. Severe bleeding

Use when: blood is spurting, flowing heavily, or will not stop.

  • Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth or gauze and keep pressing without lifting it.
  • If blood soaks through, add another cloth on top. Do not remove the first one.
  • Lay the person down and raise the injured area above the body if this does not hurt more.
  • Keep the person warm and calm while help is on the way.

Avoid: pulling out stuck objects (press around instead); washing or pushing anything into a deep wound.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D2. Difficulty breathing

Use when: the person cannot breathe normally, is gasping, or has blue lips.

  • Help the person sit upright in the position that makes breathing easiest.
  • Loosen tight clothing around the neck, chest, and waist.
  • Keep them calm and stay with them.
  • Get emergency professional care now.

Avoid: laying flat on the back; giving food or drink to someone struggling to breathe.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D3. Severe chest pain

Use when: chest pain is severe, crushing, or spreading to the arm, jaw, or back.

  • Stop all activity and sit the person upright.
  • Keep them calm and still.
  • Do not let them drive themselves.
  • Get emergency professional care now.

Avoid: waiting to see if the pain improves on its own.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D4. Unresponsive person

Use when: no response to loud voice or gentle shake.

  • Shout for help and check for a response.
  • Turn them onto their side if they are breathing, so they do not choke.
  • If they are not breathing normally, begin chest compressions if you are trained to.
  • Get emergency professional care immediately.

Avoid: giving food, drink, or medicine while not awake; leaving them alone.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D5. Seizure (fitting)

Use when: shaking, stiffening, or briefly unresponsive.

  • Clear the area around the person and cushion their head with something soft.
  • Note the time the seizure started.
  • Stay with them until the shaking stops.
  • When it stops, turn them onto their side and get professional care.

Avoid: restraining movements; putting anything in the mouth; giving water or food until fully alert.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D6. Possible stroke

Use when: sudden face drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech, or sudden confusion.

  • Note the exact time the signs started and tell the healthcare professional.
  • Get emergency professional care now.
  • Keep the person calm, sitting up, and comfortable.
  • Stay with them until help arrives.

Avoid: giving food, drink, or medicine.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D7. Severe allergic reaction

Use when: face/lip/tongue swelling, or trouble breathing after an allergen.

  • If the person has a prescribed auto-injector, help them use it.
  • Get emergency professional care now.
  • Lay them down with legs raised, unless breathing is difficult.
  • Stay with them and tell responders what happened.

Avoid: waiting to see if the swelling goes down by itself.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D8. Burns

Use when: skin burned by heat, chemicals, or sun.

  • Cool the burn under clean, cool running water for 20 minutes.
  • Remove jewellery or loose clothing near the burn, unless stuck to the skin.
  • Cover loosely with cling film or a clean cloth.
  • Get professional care for anything larger than the size of a coin, or for deep burns.

Avoid: butter, ash, oil, toothpaste, or ice; bursting blisters.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D9. Broken bone or serious injury

Use when: after a fall, limb looks wrong, or severe pain and swelling.

  • Keep the person still and support the injured limb in the position found.
  • Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for up to 20 minutes.
  • Raise the injured area if it helps and if there is no worsening pain.
  • Get professional assessment.

Avoid: straightening or pushing a bone back; walking on a possibly broken bone.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D10. Choking

Use when: cannot speak, cough, or breathe.

  • Encourage coughing if the person can still cough forcefully.
  • Give 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  • Then give up to 5 abdominal thrusts above the belly button.
  • Alternate 5 blows and 5 thrusts, and get emergency help.

Avoid: abdominal thrusts on an infant or on someone who is pregnant — seek help instead; giving water to "wash it down."

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D11. Poisoning

Use when: swallowed, inhaled, or touched a harmful substance.

  • Move the person away from the substance and into fresh air if safe.
  • Rinse the mouth with clean water if they are awake.
  • Keep the container or a photo of the substance to show responders.
  • Get emergency professional care immediately.

Avoid: inducing vomiting; giving food or drink without professional advice.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D12. Heat exhaustion or heat stroke

Use when: very hot, dizzy, weak, confused, or stopped sweating.

  • Move to shade or a cool, ventilated place.
  • Loosen clothing and cool the skin with a wet cloth.
  • Sip cool water slowly if fully awake.
  • Get help if no improvement within 30 minutes or getting worse.

Avoid: leaving the person alone in the heat.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D13. Dehydration

Use when: very thirsty, dry mouth, dark or very little urine, dizziness.

  • Rest in shade.
  • Small frequent sips of water or ORS.
  • Cool skin with a damp cloth.
  • Get professional care if fluids won't stay down or no improvement.

Avoid: strong coffee or alcohol.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak

Comment:

D14. Thoughts of harming yourself

Use when: talking about ending life or self-harm.

  • Stay with the person and take them seriously.
  • Remove means of harm if safe.
  • Contact a professional or emergency service now.
  • Do not leave them alone until help takes over.

Avoid: promising secrecy or leaving them alone.

Verdict: ☐ Correct / ☐ Too strong / ☐ Too weak
