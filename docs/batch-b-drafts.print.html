1. Sepsis warning signs — new emergency pathway (Reviewers 2 + 3 flagged it missing)

DRAFT — triggers

Patient words matching any of: fever with confusion or disorientation; fast or difficult breathing with fever; cold, pale, clammy or mottled skin with illness; feeling extremely weak with fever; much less urine than usual with fever.

DRAFT — guidance shown

These signs together can mean a serious infection spreading in the body. Get emergency professional care now. Do not wait. Keep the person comfortable and lying down if faint; do not give food or drink if confused or drowsy.

2. Serious child illness — new emergency triggers (Reviewers 2 + 3 flagged it missing)

DRAFT — triggers

Child who will not drink or breastfeed; child who vomits everything; child with fits or seizures; child unusually sleepy, floppy, or unconscious; sunken eyes in a sick child.

DRAFT — guidance shown

A child with any of these signs needs emergency professional care now. Do not wait. Keep the child with you, keep them warm, and do not force food or drink if they cannot swallow or are drowsy.

3. Seizures during pregnancy — distinct pathway (Reviewers 2 + 3 flagged it missing)

DRAFT — triggers

Seizure or fitting in a person who is pregnant.

DRAFT — guidance shown

A seizure during pregnancy is an emergency. Get emergency professional care now. Lay her on her left side if possible, do not restrain her, do not put anything in her mouth, and tell the responders she is pregnant and how far along she is.

6. Heat-stroke split — full copy (Reviewers 2 + 4; red-flag line already shipped in Batch A)

DRAFT — triggers (emergency)

Confusion, fainting, collapse, very hot dry skin, or stopped sweating during heat exposure.

DRAFT — guidance shown

This may be heat stroke, which is an emergency. Get emergency professional care immediately and cool aggressively: move to shade, remove excess clothing, pour cool water over the body and fan the skin, place wrapped ice or cold packs on the neck, armpits, and groin. Do not give fluids to a person who is confused or unconscious.

DRAFT — heat exhaustion (kept as routine self-care)

Dizziness, weakness, or heavy sweating in heat without confusion or fainting: rest in shade, loosen clothing, sip cool water slowly if fully awake, get help if no improvement within 30 minutes.

7. Choking — full age-specific algorithms (Reviewer 2; technique counts already shipped in Batch A)

DRAFT — adult or child over 1 year

Encourage forceful coughing. Give 5 firm back blows, then up to 5 abdominal thrusts above the belly button. Alternate, and get emergency help.

DRAFT — baby under 1 year

Lay the baby face down along your forearm, head lower than the chest, supporting the jaw. Give 5 firm back blows. If the object does not come out, turn the baby face up and give 5 chest thrusts with two fingers on the breastbone. Alternate, and get emergency help.

DRAFT — pregnancy

Give 5 back blows, then 5 chest thrusts on the breastbone instead of abdominal thrusts, and get emergency help.

DRAFT — person collapses

Call for emergency help, lay the person flat, and begin chest compressions as for an unresponsive person who is not breathing normally. Look in the mouth for the object before rescue breaths if you are trained.

DRAFT — cough follow-up question

9. Burns severity rule — replaces the "coin" rule (Reviewer 3 criteria)

DRAFT — replacement step

Get emergency care for burns that are deep (white, charred, or numb skin), larger than the person's palm, on the face, hands, feet, joints, or genitals, that go all the way round a limb, or caused by chemicals or electricity. Burns with soot around the nose or mouth, a hoarse voice, or coughing after smoke or fire are an emergency — the airway may be affected.

10. ORS preparation recipe (Reviewers 3 + 4 demand it)

DRAFT — added to dehydration guidance

To make oral rehydration solution: mix 1 litre of clean water with 6 level teaspoons of sugar and half a level teaspoon of salt. Stir until dissolved. It should taste no saltier than tears. Throw away leftover solution after 24 hours. Give small, frequent sips, and continue breastfeeding infants.
